OSI Layer 1: Copper and Fibre Optic Cables
● Physical layer: moves raw bits as electrical, light or radio signals
● Defines cables, connectors, voltages, wavelengths and speeds
● Devices: hubs, repeaters, media converters, transceivers (SFP)

Copper (twisted pair)
● Bits sent as electrical signals over 4 twisted pairs, RJ45 connector
● Twisting cancels crosstalk and electromagnetic interference (EMI)
● UTP: unshielded, cheap, most common; STP: shielded, for noisy environments
● Cat5e: 1 Gbps, Cat6: 10 Gbps up to 55 m, Cat6a: 10 Gbps up to 100 m
● Max segment length 100 m
● Straight-through: different device types (PC to switch); crossover: same type (switch to switch)
● Can carry power to devices (PoE)

Fibre optic
● Bits sent as light pulses through a glass core
● Immune to EMI, much longer distances and higher bandwidth
● Multimode (MMF): wide core, LED/VCSEL light, cheaper, up to ~550 m (inside buildings and data centers)
● Single-mode (SMF): narrow core, laser light, 10 km and more (between buildings and cities)
● Connectors: LC, SC, ST; usually one strand to transmit, one to receive
● More expensive and fragile, no PoE

● Typical layer 1 problems: damaged cable, bad connector, wrong cable type, length exceeded, speed/duplex mismatch
