# LAN and WAN
- Networks are classified by the area they cover and by who owns the infrastructure
- LAN: you own it; WAN: you rent connectivity from a provider

## LAN (Local Area Network)
- Covers one room, floor, building or campus
- Owned and managed by the organization itself
- High speed (1–10 Gbps and more), low latency, low error rate
- Technologies: Ethernet (wired), Wi-Fi (WLAN)
- Devices: switches, access points; a router at the edge
- Usually private IP addresses, one or more VLANs

## WAN (Wide Area Network)
- Connects LANs across cities, countries or continents
- Links are leased from an ISP or carrier, paid per bandwidth
- Lower speed, higher latency and cost than a LAN
- Technologies: fibre leased lines, MPLS, broadband, cellular (4G/5G), satellite, site-to-site VPN, SD-WAN
- Devices: routers, modems, firewalls
- The internet is the largest WAN

## LAN vs WAN

| | LAN | WAN |
|---|-----|-----|
| Area | building or campus | cities, countries |
| Ownership | the organization | service provider |
| Speed | high | lower |
| Latency | low (< 1 ms) | higher (tens of ms and more) |
| Cost | one-time equipment | recurring fees |
| Main device | switch | router |

## Other network types
- PAN: personal area, a few metres (Bluetooth, USB)
- WLAN: wireless LAN (Wi-Fi)
- CAN: campus area, several buildings of one organization
- MAN: metropolitan area, a city
- SAN: storage area network, connects servers to storage
